Streaming apps push whatever is already popular, and the producer rarely gets a mention. earlyworld does the opposite. It is a quieter place for underground music, where discovery runs on real taste and credit goes to everyone who made the record.

Producers get their name on the work

Every track lists the producer next to the artist. You can open a producer's full catalog and follow them the same way you follow an artist. Credits fill in automatically when a track is added, and anything you enter yourself takes priority.

Rare saves find your people

When you save a track it counts toward your taste, and the fewer people who saved it, the more it says about you. earlyworld matches you with listeners who saved the same hard-to-find tracks, and shows you exactly which ones you have in common.

Rate what you hear

Rate songs and releases in half-stars, add a short review if you feel like it, and pin your favorites to your profile. Your ratings build a history that tells people who you are, next to the community average for each track.

It sends you to the real source

There is no player inside earlyworld. Every track opens on its original source, so the play lands where it should and the artist and producer get credit for it. No ripped audio, no autoplay, no listening history sold on.

How it works

  1. Pick the scenes and artists you already follow.
  2. Save five tracks to start your profile.
  3. Rate what you hear and add any producer credits that are missing.
  4. Get matched with listeners who saved the same rare tracks.

It is built for people who care about music past the charts: listeners who want their finds to count, producers who want their name on their records, and anyone building a profile other people actually trust for new sounds.

Questions

Can I play music in the app?

No. earlyworld links out to each track's original source instead, so artists and producers get the play. There is no built-in player and no autoplay.

What makes a save "rare"?

Rarity is just how few people have saved a track. A shared rare save counts for more in matching than a popular one, so finding something early is what connects you to the right people.

Where do the producer credits come from?

They are filled in carefully when a track is added, and any credit you enter yourself wins. When a credit is unknown it stays blank rather than being guessed.
